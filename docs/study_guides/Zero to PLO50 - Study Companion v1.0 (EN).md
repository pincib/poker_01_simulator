## FOLLOW THE SEQUENCE
The short route tells you where you are going. For study,
use the sequence and selected clips here. Work through
the lessons in order; revisit them when your own hands
reveal a gap.
## ONE STUDY BLOCK
Plan about 30-60 minutes: learn the selected part, practise,
then check. That is study time, not the duration of the
video. Some lessons need more than one block.
## REPEAT IN REAL HANDS
Finishing a clip is not completing a skill. Repeat the drills on
another day and use marked hands from your current
games.
## BLUE TITLES ARE CLICKABLE
Time ranges tell you where to start and STOP. The link
opens at the start; YouTube will keep playing beyond the
selected end unless you stop it.
## REFERENCE AND FEEDBACK
For factual drills, check pages 28-29. For strategy,
compare the exact scenario with a suitable reference or a
peer/coach. Ask about uncertainty; do not invent an
answer.
## KEEP THE FORMAT CLEAR
NLHE means two-card Hold'em; PLO here means
four-card, six-max cash. Keep tournament, heads-up and
five-card advice separate. No purchase is required by this
syllabus.
Notation in drills: A = ace, K = king, Q = queen, J = jack, T = ten. Suits: ♠ spades, ♥ hearts, ♦ diamonds, ♣ clubs. 100bb = one hundred big blinds.

## 01 Rankings
Full house; straight; AA-KK-Q; A-2-3-4-5 is a
straight; Q-K-A-2-3 is not a straight. A royal flush is
the highest straight flush.
02 Actions and streets
No checking when facing an unmatched bet. The
last remaining player wins without showing the best
hand. Streets: preflop, flop, turn, river.
## 03 Position
BTN acts last in both CO vs BTN and BTN vs BB.
With six players, UTG acts first preflop; the first
active player left of BTN acts first postflop.
04 Why bet?
The intended goals are value in (a), bluff in (b), and
usually checking in (c). A goal still needs evidence
about the opponent and the price.
05 Price of a call
## $6: 6/(12+6+6)=25%. $12: 12/(12+12+12)=33.3%.
$4: 4/(12+4+4)=20%. Ignore rake in these
exercises.
06 Expected value
EV = 0.5 x $4 - 0.5 x $2 = +$1 per flip. Two tails lose
$4, but the offer is still profitable in expectation.
More trials do not guarantee recovery on a deadline.
Lessons 07-10 use a reference or a review, not a single universal poker action. Keep the hand details and your reason so another person can check
them.

## A♦ A♣.
12 Pot-sized raise. (a) $12. (b) Call $6, making a $24 pot; raise
$24 more, for $30 total. (c) Call $2, making a $5 pot; raise $5
more, for $7 total.
15 Aces. On A♠ 9♣ 4♦ you have top set. On K♠ Q♠ J♥ you have
only an overpair, with no spade draw; opponents can hold
straights, sets, two pair and strong draws. Your aces alone do
not choose the action.
16 Changing nuts. JT makes the nut straight on 9♠ 8♠ 7♦. The
Q♠ turn makes a flush possible for an opponent with two
spades; a straight is no longer the nuts. Always account for
exactly two hole cards.
17 Flush draws. A♠ 5♠ Q♥ J♣ has the nut flush draw; Q♠ J♠ 7♥
6♣ has a queen-high flush draw; A♠ Q♥ J♦ 5♣ has only one
spade and no immediate flush draw.
18 Wrap count. First: K, Q, J and 8 give 3+3+3+4 = 13
straight-making cards. Second: Q, J and T give 3+3+3 = 9.
Neither count means guaranteed wins by the river.
19 Dirty outs. K♠, Q♠, J♠ and 8♠ can complete a flush. The
other nine straight-making turn cards are non-spades. An
opponent can still improve on the river, and ranges determine
the full equity.
20 SPR. First: $48 effective, SPR 4. Second: $40 effective,
SPR 4. Equal SPR does not imply an equal decision; cards,
ranges, position and action still matter.
22 Blocker. No opponent ace-high spade flush because you
hold A♠. A king-high spade flush is possible. You have no flush
with only one spade. The blocker does not force folds or justify
an automatic bluff.
